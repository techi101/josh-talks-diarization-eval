What the numbers taught me

21.4% → 11.4%

The same pyannote output scores almost half the error under the old NIST rules (250 ms collar, overlap skipped). So scoring settings move numbers as much as switching systems does. Try the Scoring filter above.

2 of 4 people

On TS3003a far-field, pyannote found only 2 of the 4 speakers but still scored 26.1% DER. JER was 70%. A low DER can hide a missing person, so JER and speakers found sit right next to DER.

+6 vs +15 points

Going from headset to far-field mic cost pyannote about 6 points but live Sortformer about 15. Streaming systems break hardest on hard audio, so they get their own track.

CI −17.4 to +6.8

My baseline S2 beat the 1-speaker floor by 5 points on average, but the bootstrap can't separate them on 6 recordings. pyannote vs Sortformer offline (−10.6, CI −14.6 to −6.3) is a real gap.

How it is scored

  • DER (diarization error rate) = (missed speech + false alarm + wrong speaker) ÷ total reference speech. Lower is better. The table shows the plain mean over recordings.
  • Strict by default: 0 ms collar (no free pass at speaker changes), overlapping speech is scored, and systems are not told the speaker count.
  • JER (Jaccard error rate) averages the error per speaker, so a system that ignores the quiet person gets caught.
  • Speakers found = how many speakers the system output, against the true 4. Green when it is exactly right.
  • RTF (real-time factor) = processing time ÷ audio length, measured on a Kaggle GPU. Baselines ran on CPU and weren't timed.
  • Rank groups: sorted by DER, then each system is compared with the current group leader by a paired bootstrap of time-weighted DER. A new group starts only when the 95% interval of the difference is entirely above 0.
  • Reference: pyannote AMI-diarization-setup word-aligned RTTM. Baselines: S1 = Silero VAD with everything as one speaker (the floor). S2 = Silero VAD + ECAPA embeddings (1.5 s windows) + clustering, with the threshold tuned on a separate dev meeting (ES2011a), never on test.
  • Systems: pyannote speaker-diarization community-1; NVIDIA Sortformer v2 streaming at a 30 s buffer (offline-like) and a 1.04 s buffer (live).
The full design (60 hours of new hidden audio in 5 settings, Indian English, Hindi and Hinglish, two annotators, vendor preview, 20% refresh every 6 months) is in the design prototype. Its numbers are illustrative; the numbers on this page are not.
